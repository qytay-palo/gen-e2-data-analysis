# PS-004 Headcount Forecasting Narrative

PS-004 selected champion models by the documented rule set: lowest validation MAPE by profession, RMSE as the tie-breaker, and a simplicity preference only when the MAPE gap was negligible. That process favored ARIMA(1,1,2) for doctors and nurses, ARIMA(1,1,0) for physiotherapists, and a linear model for pharmacists. The split outcome is sensible. ARIMA won where the historical series showed enough structure for a time-series model to reduce forecast error materially, while the linear model remained the better choice for pharmacists because it produced the lowest validation error without adding unnecessary complexity.

The 2020-2024 forecast points in one direction across all four professions: continued headcount growth. Doctors are projected to rise from about 13.4k to 15.8k, nurses from 42.0k to 47.1k, pharmacists from 3.47k to 4.10k, and physiotherapists from 1.84k to 2.41k. Nurses drive the largest absolute increase, while physiotherapists show the steepest proportional lift from a smaller base. No profession is forecast to flatten or decline over the five-year horizon.

Confidence in the projections is strong for Demo 2 because every champion model met the acceptance threshold of MAPE below 10%, with observed validation error between 1.54% and 2.98%. The prediction intervals widen over time, especially for larger workforces such as nurses, so these outputs should be read as directional supply signals rather than precise staffing commitments. For Demo 2, the business implication is clear: the forecasting layer is reliable enough to feed PS-005 dashboard integration and to support an executive narrative of broad workforce supply growth, while still signaling where future planning should monitor uncertainty and base-size differences by profession.
